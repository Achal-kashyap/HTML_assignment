HTML Entities - Practice Answers

Question 1: Reserved Characters (<, >, &)

Named entities:

To create a paragraph in HTML, you write <p>your text</p>.

Closing tags always begin with </, such as in </div>.

Salt & pepper is a classic combination, just like HTML & CSS.

Hexadecimal entities:

The less-than sign can also be written as <, for example <span>.

The greater-than sign in hex form is >, as in <img />.

The ampersand in hex form is &, seen in phrases like "Rock & Roll".

Question 3: Mathematical Statements

5 ≠ 6, since five is not equal to six.

The formula ∑ i represents the sum of all integers from 1 to n. (hex entity: ∑)

The sequence approaches ∞ as n grows without bound, meaning it tends toward infinity.

Question 10: Mini Symbol Reference Page Section

Symbol Reference Guide

1. Reserved Characters: In HTML, tags are written using < and >, and the & symbol starts every character entity.

2. Currency Symbols: Prices around the world use € (Europe), £ (UK), and ¥ (Japan).

3. Mathematical Symbols: Equations may include ≠ (not equal), ∑ (summation), and ∞ (infinity).

4. Arrows: Navigation often uses ← for back, → for next, and ↑ for scrolling to the top.

5. Emojis / Legal Symbols: Pages may end with © 2026 Our Company®, alongside a friendly 😀 to welcome readers.
